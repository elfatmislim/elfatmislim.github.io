Finally, an Award People Actually Care About

Slim El Fatmi · May 1, 2026

Nobody cares about awards in Tunisia.

Not really. Not in the way that changes behavior or makes someone walk into a restaurant and say "I heard they won something." The "Élu Meilleur Service" plaque on your insurance company's wall speaks to no one. It sits there, next to a potted plant that might also be fake.

The problem is not credibility. The problem is relevance.

Sensemaking only matters when it leads to choicemaking. Nobody picks a tomato paste because it won a trophy. Nobody switches banks because of a certificate on LinkedIn. The information exists, technically, but it connects to nothing in how people actually decide.

Then Torschi came along.

The Right Category Changes Everything

Food in Tunisia is a social act, a weekly ritual, a source of genuine anxiety and genuine joy. Where to eat, what to recommend, what to avoid, this is information people actively search for, argue about, and remember. We do not have Yelp. We have Facebook groups and that one friend who seems to have eaten everywhere.

Torschi did not create an award. They created a map for choicemaking that people were already desperate to have.

That is the insight: you do not earn relevance by being rigorous. You earn it by inserting yourself into a decision people are already making.

The Execution Is a Gem

I will say this once: the Torschi visual identity should be studied in Tunisian marketing programs.

Not because it is pretty. Because it is coherent.

From the logo to the typography to every touchpoint, everything speaks the same language. And in a market where most brands cannot maintain visual consistency between their Instagram post and their storefront, that alone is remarkable.

But coherence is the floor, not the ceiling. What makes it genuinely worth studying is that every aesthetic decision is load-bearing. Nothing is decorative. The colors do not just look good, they signal something. The typography does not just feel nice, it positions something. When design works at that level, you are not looking at branding anymore. You are looking at strategy made visible.

Tunisia has almost no examples of this. We have logos. We have color palettes. We rarely have systems.

Torschi built a system.

Killing the Corporate Award

Corporate awards feel like a cult.

Not metaphorically. Structurally. The hierarchy is invisible but present. The selection criteria are stated but never really explained. Everyone claps at the right moment. Nobody asks questions. And somewhere in your mind, a small voice says: the decision was made before you arrived.

It does not matter if that is true. Feeling fake is exactly as damaging as being fake.

Torschi dismantled this by changing the entire emotional register. The colors, the art direction, the atmosphere, it lands closer to a kindergarten end-of-year ceremony than a Chamber of Commerce dinner.

And then the decisive move: they made the hero the owner, not the brand.

A real person. A real story. Because people trust people in ways they will never trust plaques. The moment you put a human being at the center, the whole cult architecture collapses.

This is what good marketing looks like when it stops trying to impress and starts trying to matter.
